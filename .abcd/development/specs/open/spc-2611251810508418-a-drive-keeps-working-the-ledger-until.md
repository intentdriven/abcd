# A drive keeps working the ledger until the time the person named

## Summary

This spec delivers itd-2611251809580662. `abcd drain --drive` runs until an end time, seven days on by default, begins each next drain itself, and waits on an empty ledger. Each drain holds a set number of lanes in work, one by default, and takes its next issue as soon as the lane in hand has queued its pull request. One person's ceiling on live reservations is read from the repository, so every machine reads the same number. Each drive publishes its state as one fixed-form commit on the person's status branch, under a label the person chose, and `abcd status` and the board show everything that is one person's in one place. The start says that a reboot, a logout or a crash ends the drive and prints the one command that carries on.

Every product decision is in the intent (decisions 1 to 6, the product thinker's, 2026-11-25) and every consequence settled without a question is beside them (decisions 7 to 13). The spec opens no question.

Seven steps, each one pull request. Steps 1 and 2 are the reservation limits; they edit no Go file that spc-2611141617199772 edits and wait on none of its steps. The only files they share with it are `commands/drain.md` and the brief's drain page, which that spec's step 5 also edits in other sections. Steps 3 to 7 edit `internal/core/implement/loop/terminal.go` or `internal/surface/cli/drain_drive.go`, so none starts before steps 1 to 4 of spc-2611141617199772 are on the default branch, and they land one at a time in order. Step 7 also waits on that spec's step 5, which adds the board's reader of a drive's state.

One flag is new (`--in-work`), three configuration keys are new (`drain.in_work_max`, `drive.until_days`, `drive.label`), one key gains a layer (`reservation.max_live` in the repository), and no command is new. The drain state's schema version rises by one in step 2 and the run-state schema does not change. No dependency is added.

## Approach

### What is on the default branch today

- **The ceiling.** `maxLive` (`internal/core/claim/maxlive.go`) reads `reservation.max_live` from the machine's `~/.abcd.noindex/config.json` alone, ten when absent, 1 to 100. The reservation act (`internal/core/claim/crowd.go`), the ceiling view of `abcd peers` (`CeilingFor`, `internal/core/claim/ceiling.go`) and the drain's wait at the cap (`MaxLiveFor`, called at `internal/core/implement/loop/drain.go`) all read it. The count is over the person: `Self.Owns` (`internal/core/claim/holderid.go`) answers for the account's holder id, the ids declared in `~/.abcd.noindex/holder-ids`, and the login on a hold made before the id.
- **The gate on a drain's next lane.** `drainMove` (`drain.go`) returns "drive this lane, one lane at a time" while any lane it opened is in progress, and a lane whose landing has armed its merge reads as `DrainLanePullRequest` (`laneOutcome`), so the move goes on to the next issue. One lane in work is therefore what a drain does today, with no setting and no name for it. The only number in the gate is the person's ceiling, counted before each reservation.
- **A layered read at a commit.** `layered.Roots.RepoAt` reads the repository layer from the object store at a named commit, never from the working tree, with `RepoAtAsStored` for a reader that takes no replace ref (`internal/core/layered/layered.go`). The merge gate reads `facilitator.mode` this way (`internal/core/landing/config.go`). `originDefault` (`internal/core/claim/remote.go`) names origin's default branch as the checkout last fetched it.
- **A repository figure that may only lower.** `resolveBundleMax` (`internal/core/implement/loop/drain_bundle.go`) holds `drain.bundle_max` to the machine's figure and names a repository figure it ignored. It claims the `drain` namespace with one key.
- **The drive's bound.** `parseDriveUntil` (`internal/surface/cli/drain_drive.go`) reads `--until`; with none, each wait ends the drive after eight hours (`terminalOutageLimit` and the `idle` rule, `terminal.go` and `terminal_wait.go`). `driveDefaultCap` is eight lanes, and a `--max` that differs from a drain in progress is refused. `driveConsent` prints the bound before the question.
- **The drive's endings.** `terminal.run` ends the drive on any `res.Stopped` of a drain's move: `DrainStoppedCap` and `DrainStoppedEmpty` among them. A new drain begins at the next move after one has ended: `drainMove` with no live state resolves the pace and the bundle cap again and writes a new state over the old.
- **The drive's re-execution.** A drive that takes a release re-executes with its own arguments (`driveArgs`, `internal/surface/cli/drain_drive_update.go`) and the hidden `--after-update`. `f.resuming()` tells such a start from one a person typed, and a resuming start asks nothing.
- **The status branch.** An announcement is one empty-tree commit of fixed trailers on `refs/heads/abcd-status/<holder id>` (`internal/core/claim/announce.go`), read through one intake (`internal/core/claim/status.go`) that validates each commit against its family and keeps only validated fields. A commit that does not read is shown as doubtful by its branch and reason, never by its text.
- **What is not there yet.** `DriveState`, its file `.abcd/.work.local/logs/drive-state.json` and the board's reader of it are steps 4 and 5 of spc-2611141617199772. Steps 5 to 7 here build on them and do not define them.

### The ceiling is the repository's (step 1; criteria 1 to 3; decisions 3, 7 and 8)

`maxLive` takes the checkout's root as well as the home and resolves in this order:

1. The bundled ten.
2. The repository's `reservation.max_live`, read from `.abcd/config.json` at the commit `originDefault(root)` names, as stored (`RepoAt`, `RepoAtAsStored`), with the machine layer left out of that load (`HomeRefused`, as `landing/config.go` does). Where it is set it replaces the ten.
3. The machine's `reservation.max_live`, read as today. Below the figure so far, it replaces it. Above it, it is ignored and named in the note the ceiling view and the cap's refusal already carry. Equal, it changes nothing.

Rules that keep this safe:

- The repository layer is never read from a working tree. A checkout with no origin, or one that has never fetched origin's default branch, reads no repository figure and says so in the note; the bundled ten and the machine's figure apply.
- In the repository's file the `reservation` namespace admits `max_live` and nothing else. `github_username` and `fork_origin` are the person's own and are refused there, naming the file and the commit: a repository must not be able to say who a person is or where their fork lives. The machine's load keeps its three keys (`reservationKeys`, `internal/core/claim/holder.go`).
- A repository value that is not a whole number from 1 to 100 refuses an act that adds a reservation, naming `.abcd/config.json at origin/<branch>`, as a bad machine value does today. It refuses no release and no renewal.
- `Ceiling` gains `LimitLayer` and `LimitOrigin` (the layer that set the number and where), and `CapError` gains the same, so the refusal's advice names the place the number can be changed: the repository's file through a reviewed change, or the machine's file to lower it. The sentence that told a person to raise the machine's key is removed.
- `MaxLiveFor` takes the root. Its one caller outside the package is the drain's move.
- The comment at the head of `maxlive.go`, which says the setting is the machine's and never a checkout's, is rewritten to the new order and names the intent's decision 3. That decision refines dcn-2611222342270127: the bundle cap's rule there is untouched.

`abcd peers` prints the ceiling row as today with the layer beside the number, for example `your holds: you 7 of 10 (repository) (this copy 2, laptop 3, elsewhere 2)`, and `--json` carries `limit_layer` and `limit_origin`. Its warning names the repository's ceiling, not this machine's.

### A drain's lanes in work (step 2; criteria 4 to 6; decision 2)

A new file `internal/core/implement/loop/drain_inwork.go` holds the limit.

- **The setting.** `drain.in_work_max`, a whole number from 1 to 10. It resolves once, when a drain begins: the `--in-work <n>` flag, then the repository's figure read at origin's default branch as step 1 reads the ceiling, then the bundled one. The machine's file is not read for it: the number is the repository's and the run's, and a machine that wants fewer starts fewer drains. It is written into the drain's state with its layer and origin (`DrainState.InWorkMax`, a `PaceValue` like `BundleMax`), and a running drain takes no new figure from any file. `--in-work` given to a drain already in progress with another figure is refused as `--max` is today. `DrainSchemaVersion` rises by one (to 5 as the default branch stands; one more if another spec has raised it first); a state at the earlier version reads as a limit of one. A `drain.in_work_max` in the machine's file is refused naming the file, as a key that layer does not hold, and is never passed over in silence.
- **The namespace.** `resolveBundleMax` claims the `drain` namespace with `bundle_max` alone, so a file that sets `drain.in_work_max` would be refused there as a misspelt key. Both resolvers claim the namespace with both keys, from one list.
- **What counts.** A lane the drain opened whose outcome is `DrainLaneInProgress` and which is not in progress only for the withdrawal its hand-back owes (`owedArming`, which already holds up no other lane). A lane whose landing has armed its merge is `DrainLanePullRequest` and is not counted; it keeps its reservation until the merge, as today. A lane that carries several issues counts once.
- **The gate.** In `drainMove`, the loop over the drain's lanes counts the lanes in work. Where the count is below the limit the move goes on to reserve and open the next lane, and names every lane in work in its `Next`. Where it has reached the limit the move returns as it does today. With the default of one this is today's behaviour, now named and counted.
- **The drive.** A drive drives each lane it is handed until the lane has queued its pull request or been handed back, so a drive holds one lane in work whatever the figure. Nothing in `terminal.go` changes in this step.
- **Both counts where a person looks.** `DrainResult` gains `InWork` (the count, the limit and its layer) and `Held` (the person's live reservations and the ceiling, when the move's view could tell them). The drain's two wait sentences (`capWaitText` and the "drive this lane" sentence, `drain_wait.go` and `drain.go`) name both, for example "1 of 1 lane in work; you hold 7 of the 10 reservations the repository allows". A drive already says a wait's reason, so it shows both with no change to its own files. `abcd peers` adds a row under the ceiling row, `this drain: 1 of 1 lane in work (repository default)`, read from the drain's state through a new `loop.DrainInWork(root)`; with no drain in progress the row is absent.

### The end time and what the start says (step 3; criteria 10, 11, 12 and 14; decisions 1, 6 and 9)

- **The default.** `parseDriveUntil` returns the end for an empty `--until`: the start plus the default days. The default is seven, or `drive.until_days` from the repository's configuration at origin's default branch, a whole number from 1 to 31, resolved in a new `internal/core/implement/loop/drive_bound.go` (`ResolveDriveDays`) so the surface holds no configuration rule. A value outside the range refuses the start naming its origin. `--until` given by the person wins and is not held to the default: it is the person's own bound.
- **Every drive is bounded.** `f.until` is never zero after this step, so `terminal.bounded()` is always true: the drive waits through limits, outages and an unreadable origin until its end time, and the eight-hour endings (`TerminalWaited`, `TerminalOutage`, `TerminalLimited`) no longer end a drive a person started. Those constants and their sentences stay for `WaitOutProof` and for any caller that passes no bound; a test pins that a drive started from the command always passes one. The limit probe is always wired, and the consent's `estimate` line always carries the retry wording.
- **Fixed by the start.** The default is read once, before the consent, shown there as an instant, and covered by the yes. A re-execution passes the end as an explicit `--until <RFC 3339 instant>` in the arguments it builds (`drain_drive_update.go`), whether or not the person gave one, and a resuming start reads no default. A test re-executes a drive after the repository's default has changed and asserts the end is the first one.
- **What the start says.** The start keeps the seven lines of itd-2611141610192096 (its first criterion, ruled 2026-11-25), and this step adds none to them: spc-2611251805244246, "The start's seven lines are full", leaves to this spec where its words go (technical facilitator, 2026-11-25). The end time sits in the start's existing `ends` line, with its source as one word: `at <date and time> (default)`, `(repository)` or `(--until)`. The rest goes on `?` and in the full plan, as spc-2611251808081374 places the interrupt's line: an `ends` line with the source in full ("seven days on, abcd's default", "the repository's default of 14 days", or "--until"); a `restart` line saying that a reboot, a logout or a crash ends the drive, that nothing starts it again, and the command, `abcd drain --drive --until <the same instant>`, with `--profile <name>` when one was given; and the `holds` line below. After the yes the command is printed once, beside the log's path and the interrupt's line. The same command is what `TerminalResult.Resume` carries at an end that leaves work, so one place composes it (`driveResumeCommand`). The full plan is written to the drive's log at every start, `--yes` included.
- **Carrying on.** The command is an ordinary start: it asks its one question, takes up what the interrupt left as spc-2611141617199772 step 1 builds it, and ends at the time named. A test starts a drive, ends its process without a stop, starts the printed command and asserts the lanes go on and the end time is unchanged.
- **The flag's help and the bound line.** `--until`'s help and `commands/drain.md` stop saying "eight hours on each wait". The full plan's `holds` line names the limits beside the bound: `1 lane in work; 10 reservations per person (repository)`, read through step 2's `loop.DrainInWork` and `claim.MaxLiveFor`.

### The drive begins the next drain itself (step 4; criteria 7, 8, 11 and 13; decision 1)

- **The cap is the drive's.** `TerminalOptions` gains `MaxLanes` (zero for none) and `Opened` (the lanes this drive has opened so far, zero at a typed start). The surface passes `--max` there and no longer turns it into the drain's `Max`: a drain a drive begins has no cap of its own. `driveDefaultCap` is removed. The drive counts a lane each time a move opens one (`res.Start != nil && res.Lane != nil`), across drains, and ends with a new `TerminalCap` ("the drive's lane cap is reached") when the count reaches `MaxLanes`. A re-execution passes the count on in a hidden `--opened <n>`, refused on a start that is not resuming.
- **A drain in progress with a cap of its own.** A drain begun by an earlier release, or by a session, may carry `max`. The drive no longer refuses a differing `--max` and no longer caps an uncapped drain (`CapDrainInProgress` loses its caller and is removed). It drives that drain to its cap and then begins the next, as for any drain that ends.
- **Beginning the next.** In `terminal.run`, a move whose `Stopped` is `DrainStoppedCap` no longer ends the drive. The drive says one line, "began drain <n> of this drive: the last opened <k> lanes and ended at its cap", and its next pass calls `Drain` again, which begins a new drain because none is live. `DrainStoppedOutage` and `DrainStoppedOrigin` are given the same treatment only while the drive is bounded, which after step 3 is always: the drive waits (`idle`, at `TerminalWaitFloor`) and its next pass begins a drain. `DrainStoppedEmpty` still ends the drive in this step; step 5 changes it.
- **What the next drain keeps.** `drainMove` already carries pending hand-backs into a new drain. The pace, the in-work limit and the bundle cap are resolved again from the layers, which is right for a new drain; the flags the person gave (`--pace`, `--sub-agents`, `--fix-rounds`, `--in-work`) were "taken once" by the first move, so the drive keeps them aside and gives them to each drain it begins. A person's `--skip` lasts for the life of the drive (technical facilitator, 2026-11-25, dcn-2611252011565595 and dcn-2611252026516969): each drain the drive begins takes the ended drain's skip list exactly as its state left it, so a skip or an unskip made mid-drive with `abcd drain --skip` or `--unskip` from another terminal carries on as well as one given at the start, an empty look that ends a drain never undoes either with nobody watching, and the list clears when the drive ends. A skip still clears at the end of a drain a host session steps, as documented.
- **The ended drain's state.** Before a new drain overwrites `drain.json`, `drainMove` copies the ended state to `.abcd/.work.local/run/drains/drain-<ended-at>.json`, keeping the newest twenty. It is a copy for a postmortem; nothing reads it back.
- **A stale state, said.** Where the start finds a drain in progress, the full plan gains a `drain` line, and one line after the yes says the same: when it began, when it last moved, how many lanes it has opened, and its pace and cap; the seven lines are unchanged. Where it finds an ended one it says so and that a new drain begins. This is the part of iss-2610101617043118 and iss-2611061902550488 that concerns a drive; neither issue is resolved by it, since both also concern a bare `abcd drain`.
- **The estimate.** With no lane cap the consent's `estimate` line gives agent runs for one lane (`EstimateRuns(o, repoRoot, 1)`) and says the drive opens lanes until its end time; with `--max` it gives the total as today.
- **The end's words.** `driveEndWords` gains `TerminalCap`; "the cap is reached" stays for a drain's own cap, which now reaches a person only from a drain a session steps.

### Waiting on an empty ledger (step 5; criterion 9; decision 1)

- A move whose `Stopped` is `DrainStoppedEmpty` no longer ends the drive. The drive says "nothing is eligible; looking again at <time>" once per wait, waits fifteen minutes (`drainWaitMinutes`, the interval a drain already waits at its cap and on held work), and its next pass begins a drain, which reads origin afresh. A pass that finds nothing writes a state of a few hundred bytes and reserves and pushes nothing.
- An empty drain is not archived by step 4's copy when it opened no lane, judged nothing and handed nothing back, so a quiet night does not fill the folder with ninety-six files; the test asserts the folder is unchanged after a run of empty looks.
- The line "began drain <n>" is not said for a drain that found nothing; it is said when a drain opens its first lane or asks its first judgement.
- **The state.** In `drivestate.go` the wait sets `waiting` with `Until` at the next look, and that look-again time is a named end, as the hourly retry of set-aside lanes is (decision 9 of itd-2611141610197753): a drive on an empty ledger reads `waiting` and never `stalled`, however long the ledger stays empty. `On` is the fixed word for an empty queue.
- The drive still ends at its end time, at its lane cap and on a stop while it waits; the wait is cut at the end time as every sleep is (`terminal.sleep`).
- A drain a session steps is unchanged: it stops as empty and says so.

### The published line (step 6; criteria 15 to 17; decisions 4, 5 and 10)

- **The label.** `drive.label` in the machine's `~/.abcd.noindex/config.json`, read by `claim.DriveLabel(home)` through a machine-only load. It is 1 to 24 characters of lower-case letters, digits and hyphens, starting with a letter or digit. A label that equals the machine's host name (as `os.Hostname` gives it, and its part before the first dot) or the account's name (`os/user`), compared without case, is refused naming neither value. A `drive.label` in a repository's file is refused. With none set the label is `drive-` and the first six characters of the working copy's key (`copy-key`, 32 random hex characters that name neither a path nor a host), and the start says so and names the key to set.
- **The form.** A new family in `internal/core/claim/drivestate.go`, beside the announcement's and built on the same writer, collector and validator: one empty-tree commit on the person's status branch with the subject `abcd drive: <state> <label>` and these trailers, each written once and exactly, and nothing else:

  | Trailer | Holds |
  |---|---|
  | `Abcd-Drive` | `working`, `waiting`, `stalled` or `stopped` |
  | `Abcd-Label` | the label |
  | `Abcd-Sender` | the holder id |
  | `Abcd-Copy` | the working copy's key |
  | `Abcd-On` | one word from a fixed list: `lane`, `judgement`, `pace`, `limit`, `outage`, `origin`, `held`, `ceiling`, `in-work`, `empty`, `set-aside`, `stop`, `end-time`, `lane-cap`, `refusal`, `other` |
  | `Abcd-Item` | optional: one issue or intent id, the lane in hand |
  | `Abcd-Since` | when the state began |
  | `Abcd-Ends-At` | the drive's end time |
  | `Abcd-Counts` | four whole numbers 0 to 9999: lanes done, in work, set aside, handed back |
  | `Abcd-Behind` | optional: a whole number 1 to 9999, the commits the checkout is behind origin's default branch where a fast-forward was declined, and one word from a fixed list for what declined it |
  | `Abcd-Sent-At`, `Abcd-Next-At` | when it was written, and the time by which the drive will have written again |
  | `Assisted-by` | `abcd:<version>`, as an announcement's |

  Times are RFC 3339 in UTC to the second. The validator admits nothing but these fields at their grammar and rejects a message with any other line, as the announcement's does.
- **Nothing composed is published.** The stored `DriveState` carries `On` and `Do` as sentences, which can name a path or quote a refusal. A pure function `PublishedDrive(state)` maps the value to the fixed fields: `On` to its word, the lane's issue to `Abcd-Item`, and `Do` to nothing. The loop sets a typed reason on the state where it sets the sentence (step 4 of spc-2611141617199772 owns the value; this step adds the typed field `OnKind` to it), so no word is read back out of a sentence.
- **A declined fast-forward** (the intent's decision 13). The spec for itd-2611141610201875 adds `Checkout` to the stored state in its step 6: a count, a kind, a sentence and paths. `PublishedDrive` maps the count and the kind to `Abcd-Behind`, a kind it does not know to `other`, and the sentence, the tip and the paths to nothing. This step builds the trailer's grammar, its reader and the mapping. Where that field is not on the default branch when this step is cut, the writer leaves the trailer out, the mapping's test runs against the trailer's own type, and the lane captures an issue for the one line that reads the field, found at `internal/core/claim/drivestate.go`.
- **Who the commit says wrote it.** The author and committer are those an announcement's commit has (`prepareAnnouncement`): the person's git identity, or the forge account's when git's identity is unset or a machine's. So the `account@host` identity git composes for itself never reaches the commit.
- **When it is pushed.** The drive calls a hook, `TerminalOptions.PublishState`, each time the state file is written with a changed state word, `OnKind` or item, and at each hourly renewal of what it holds (`renewHeld`). The surface wires the hook to `claim.PublishDrive`. Two pushes are never closer than one minute, except `stopped`, which is pushed at once as the drive ends. A push that fails is said once per failure kind and tried again at the next change; it never ends or delays the drive. A checkout with no origin publishes nothing and says so once.
- **Reading it.** The intake that reads the status branches (`status.go`) recognises the family by its first trailer and keeps, per branch and copy key, only the newest drive line, in the local tier beside the kept announcements. A drive line is never counted among the announcements, never shown at a prompt, and never uses up the intake's or the kept store's caps for announcements. One whose sender is not its branch's owner is doubtful, as an announcement's is.
- **What an earlier release does with it.** Its reader finds a commit that is not an announcement and shows it as unreadable by branch and reason for a day, with none of its text. That is the cost decision 10 names.

### The view of one's own (step 7; criteria 18 to 20; decisions 4 and 7)

- **`abcd status`.** A block headed `yours (<login>)` comes first, built in `internal/surface/cli/status_mine.go` from three reads the command already makes or bounds:
  - the kept drive lines whose sender `Self.Owns`, one line each, composed by the function that composes the local line with the label in front: `laptop: stalled 2h44m · on outage · ends 2 Dec 09:00`, followed where the line carries `Abcd-Behind` by the count and the word, for example `· 12 behind (dirty)`. A line whose `Abcd-Next-At` has passed by more than the slack a renewal is given reads `stopped, not heard from since <Sent-At>`, whatever word it carries;
  - the reservations view's ceiling (`CeilingFor`, step 1) with the in-work count of each of the person's copies, told across machines from the stage each lane's renewal already carries (`Abcd-Stage`; `land` with a queued pull request is not in work), and step 2's row for this checkout's drain;
  - the person's open pull requests on the repository, asked of the forge (`gh`) within the five seconds the scheduled-workflow read already allows itself: number and state (open, queued, blocked) only, never a title or a branch name. An answer that does not come says the pull requests are unknown and changes no exit.
- **An id that is not declared.** A drive line or a live reservation under a holder id that is neither the reader's nor declared is not counted and not shown in the block. Where at least one drive line of another holder is present, the block ends with one fixed line that names `~/.abcd.noindex/holder-ids` and `abcd reserve --holder` as the way to declare an id of the person's own. The other holder's id and label are not echoed in it.
- **The board.** The reader spc-2611141617199772 step 5 adds for the local drive (`internal/surface/cli/board_drive.go`) gains the kept drive lines: the building line reads, for example, `drives: 3 yours (2 working, 1 stopped)`, and `--json` carries the lines. The board reads from disk alone, as it does for reservations; only `abcd status` fetches.
- **No flag.** The block is part of the bare command, and `--json` carries it under `mine`. Nothing is added for a person to remember.

### Trust boundaries, in one place

1. **A repository's file can change what a drive holds and how long it runs** (steps 1, 2 and 3). Bounded by: read at origin's default branch only; fixed ranges (1 to 100, 1 to 10, 1 to 31); the end shown in the start's `ends` line before the yes, the in-work limit on `?` and in the full plan, and both fixed for the run; the machine may lower the ceiling.
2. **A lane's agent can write the working tree** (steps 1 to 3). No repository figure is read from one; each step has a test that edits the working tree's `.abcd/config.json` and asserts nothing changes.
3. **What a drive publishes is public and permanent** (step 6). Bounded by a fixed form with no free text, a label rule, and tests that plant the machine's host name, the home path and the account name in every sentence of the state and assert the pushed bytes hold none of them.
4. **What a reader shows was chosen by a pusher** (steps 6 and 7). Every field is held to a grammar before it is kept, as an announcement's is, and passed through `termsafe` at render; a line whose sender is not its branch's owner is doubtful; a drive line never becomes an instruction and never appears in a prompt block.
5. **A drive never extends its own bound** (steps 3 and 4). The end and the lane count travel through a re-execution as explicit arguments; a resuming start reads no default; the hidden `--opened` is refused on a typed start.

### Tests and pages in every step

Every test named below is written first and watched to fail on the default branch as it stands when the step is cut, then to pass with the change. A lane runs its tests through the load gate, with `-trimpath`. Each step updates the command pages and brief pages it names in the same pull request, and regenerates `docs/reference/cli/commands.md` where a flag or a help text changes.

## How the Acceptance Criteria are met

The criteria are numbered in the order the intent lists them.

1. The repository's `reservation.max_live` is every machine's ceiling: `maxLive` reads it at origin's default branch (step 1). Proved by two homes with different machine files against one repository, each reading the repository's figure, and by a count that includes a lane at `land`.
2. A machine's lower figure applies and a higher one is ignored and named (step 1). Proved by both cases, the note read in `abcd peers` and in the cap's refusal.
3. A changed working tree or branch has no effect: the read is at a commit (steps 1, 2 and 3, one test each).
4. A drain opens no lane while its lanes in work stand at the limit, and takes the next once the lane's merge is armed: the gate in `drainMove` (step 2).
