The commit subjects abcd composes break the title rule, so do most recent commit subjects, and nothing checks either. The guide says a title takes a conventional prefix from a list of seven (feat, fix, docs, chore, refactor, test, ci) and no scopes (.github/CONTRIBUTING.md:106-107), and the COMMITTING rules domain repeats the no-scopes rule with six of the seven, leaving out ci (internal/core/rules/defaults/rules.json).

abcd composes commit subjects and pull-request titles of its own, and those found break the rule. The list below is what a search of the Go code, the page scripts it embeds and `scripts/` turned up, and it is not offered as complete: the fix looks for others. The implement loop composes three commit subjects on a lane branch, each for the default branch. It writes `chore(record): land …` for the records commit of a landing (internal/core/implement/loop/land.go:158), `chore(record): … picked by run …` for the pick commit of a run that `build next` started (internal/core/implement/loop/pickcommit.go:67; no commit behind 71e07e7ea carries it yet), and `merge: sync …` for the merge commit that brings a lane up to date (internal/core/implement/loop/sync.go:49-53), whose prefix is not among the seven. The cloud hand-back composes `chore(cloud): hand back the report on …` (scripts/cloud/hand-back.sh:348), which never reaches the default branch: it is pushed to the work-in-progress branch of the session, the collect leaves every report commit out of what it carries over (scripts/cloud/collect.sh), and a required check refuses a pull request that comes from such a branch or adds a report (scripts/check-wip-source.sh). The page of a live brief session composes `Edit the brief in a live session`, with no conventional prefix: it is the subject of the one commit the page makes through the forge, where no hook runs, and the title of the pull request it opens into the branch the session read (internal/surface/session/web/session.js:287, 2628 and 2647); no commit behind 71e07e7ea carries it yet. The pull-request titles of the loop, `fix(<issue id>): …` and `build(<intent id>): …` (land.go:2224 and 2226), break the same rule, and build is not among the seven; composing those titles is iss-2610080546203039, whose remedy already drops the scope; iss-2611101318528163 is open on the same composer for a different fault, the markdown of a step's text carried into the title; and an open spec plans a further scoped title for the loop, in its design of the landing of a bundle (spc-2611201455051897; its first step has landed, the step that covers the landing of a bundle has not, and the loop composes only the two titles above). Not counted as composed here: the commits abcd writes for reservations, announcements and its own stores, which stay on refs or in repositories of their own; the rehearsal commits of the release workflow, made on a scratch branch; the re-authored dependency bump, whose subject the script carries over from the bot unchanged (scripts/dependency-reauthor.sh); and the title `abcd land` proposes for a pull request, which is the first commit subject of the branch (internal/core/landing/prbody.go).

Of the 400 most recent non-merge commits behind 71e07e7ea on the default branch, 241 carry a scope: 79 open with `chore(record): land`, the subject of the records commit of a landing (the loop composed 77 of them, and two were written by hand in the same shape), 105 are the other commits of the lane pull requests of the loop, written inside its lanes (`docs(brief)`, `fix(matrix)`, `docs(<intent id>)`), and 57 came through other pull requests (`fix(<intent id>)`, `fix(interview)`, `fix(loop)`). Five more carry a prefix outside the seven (`brief:`, `records:`, `sweep:`) and three carry no conventional prefix, one of them the subject git writes for a revert. The lane briefs say where to commit and nothing about the shape of a title (internal/core/implement/loop/brief.go, issuebrief.go), and the brief of a cloud session says nothing about it either (scripts/cloud/brief.md.tmpl).

No hook or gate reads the shape of a title, so the rule and the practice drifted apart with no signal. The places that judge every commit message today are the commit-message hook and two gates. git runs the hook for a commit and for a merge that creates one; a commit made with the hook skipped, a rebase, a cherry-pick, an applied patch or a message edited on the forge goes round it, as .githooks/commit-msg says of its own reach. The two gates are the commits form of scripts/check-attribution.sh and that of scripts/check-issue-resolution.sh: preflight runs both over the commits of a branch, and CI runs both over those of a pull request and of a merge-queue entry, the first also over each push to the default branch. The second leaves merge commits out; the first reads the message of a merge for its outbound check and not for its trailer rules. The first is also called by the pre-push hook over the commits of a push to a work-in-progress branch (.githooks/pre-push) and by the collect over the commits before the last report commit (scripts/cloud/collect.sh), so a check placed there judges the commits of a cloud session, the hand-back's among them; the second is called in neither place, though the preflight the collect then runs applies both to the commits it carried over. A pull-request title never passes the commit-message hook: CI reads one in the pr form of scripts/check-issue-resolution.sh, in a step that skips a pull request a bot opened, and in this repository the shell guard reads the title a `gh pr` command writes before it posts (internal/core/guard/forge.go). Checking the shape of a title belongs to this record; iss-2610080546203039 only recomposes the titles the loop writes.

A check that knew only the list would refuse what today's practice produces, in at least these cases. Merge commits, unless it leaves them out: git and the forge write most of their subjects (`Merge pull request …`, `Merge remote-tracking branch …`), and the sync brief of the loop tells its implementer to run a plain `git merge` (internal/core/implement/loop/sync.go). The revert: the command the guide gives (.github/CONTRIBUTING.md:100-102) leaves the subject git writes, `Revert …`, and the revert trace of the landings recognises a revert in part by that subject (internal/core/landings/revert.go). The breaking marker: twelve subjects behind 71e07e7ea carry `!` after the prefix or the scope (`feat!:`). The dependency bump: the re-author keeps the subject of the bot, .github/dependabot.yml sets no prefix for it, and the three subjects the bot has written in this history carry a scope (`chore(deps): …`). The pull-request titles of the loop until iss-2610080546203039 has landed, since the pr form runs in a required check. The commit and the pull request of a live brief session until both are recomposed, since the person at the page words neither. A run of the loop that made its pick or records commit under the old subject and resumes on a binary that composes the new one: the loop recognises both commits by their exact subject (land.go, pickcommit.go). A scoped commit already pushed on an open branch, which the ruleset against rewriting history keeps from being reworded (.abcd/work/rulesets/no-history-rewriting.json). And a check placed in the commit-message hook, which judges other repositories too where a global dispatcher runs the hook for every clone on the machine: the hook confines its record check to its own repository by comparing git common directories (.githooks/commit-msg), and a title check there needs the same. The composed subjects are not confined to this repository either: the subjects of the loop, the lane briefs and the bundled COMMITTING domain ship in the binary, so the subjects of the loop break the bundled rule wherever a repository has not changed it, and since a repository may override that domain in its own `.abcd/rules.json`, a brief that states the rule states the one in force there. Allowing scopes instead would be a change of the rule, made in the guide, the rules domain and the remedy of iss-2610080546203039 together.
