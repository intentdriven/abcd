With the technical facilitator on auto, sessions still stop and wait, and the setting by itself arms nothing that tells a session it may not. abcd has those checks: the away mode (itd-2610180912189125, shipped) refuses a question, checks a turn's end and repeats the rule at every prompt, and it answered the first incident of this kind (iss-2610170619247185). They hang on the away record and not on the facilitator setting, and on 2026-11-21 no away record held until a session set one at 17:27 UTC. That day one session planning an intent waited three ways. It held the product thinker's sign-off behind a review it had started itself, and ended three turns on a status line while that review ran. After every line of the draft had been confirmed, it left the sign-off for the person's return, because the intent page reserves the plan act to the person and the ruling of 2026-11-07 (dcn-2611070734169709) lets a reserved act wait. And it did not act on the coordinating session's relay that the person was away, since a relay is not the person's word. The person had to come back and say it again, the third time after 17 October and 7 November. The facilitator setting is one key of the configuration, facilitator.mode. The merge gate reads it, from the default branch's committed copy and the machine's layer and never from the working tree; no hook reads it when a session stops or asks, the rules loader sends nothing about it, and abcd mode prints managed whatever it is set to. The guard refuses five acts as the person's own, and an approving review on the forge besides; the plan act is not among them, and the rest of what is reserved to the person is prose on the command pages. The rule that a session on auto does not wait lives in one decision and in each session's local notes.

What the lab found (lab-261121170045-71e07e7, at commit 71e07e7ea, the same day). The checks this record asked for already exist, and they hang on another switch. A shipped intent (itd-2610180912189125) refuses every question, checks every turn end and repeats the rule at every prompt, while the away record holds. Paired runs of the hooks, with the facilitator on auto in both halves, show that without that record the same question is admitted and the same turn end is allowed, in silence. The question in those runs carried a header outside the gate's chip grammar, which the gate admits unchecked in every state but away. What the gate does with a question addressed to the technical facilitator by its chip was not run, and a probe for it is owed before the probes serve as the fix's test. Neither the question gate nor the stop check reads the facilitator setting. The away record set on 2026-11-07 had ended at its seven-day cap on 2026-11-14, so the autonomous run that followed had neither check until a session set the record again on 2026-11-21 at 17:27 UTC, on the person's word that they were away. The person names the facilitator setting as the reason a session should not wait; abcd has two switches where they see one. The remedy above closes the part that belongs to the facilitator setting, and three records hold the rest: iss-2611211733065807, iss-2611211733085834 and iss-2611211733098545.
