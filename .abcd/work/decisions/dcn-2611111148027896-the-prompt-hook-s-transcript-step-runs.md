2026-11-11 — A hook's transcript drain finishes, in that same pass, every staged entry it begins, part after part, until a bound below the host's limit for that hook, counted from the binary's start; past the bound it starts no part and no entry, keeps the parts it finished on disk, and exits 0, and the next pass continues from the parts kept (facilitator-on-delegation, iss-2611101302324425). The prompt hook's bound is 110 seconds, its 120-second limit less a 10-second margin; the session start's is 220 seconds, its 240-second limit less a 20-second margin. The limits are named constants a test holds to hooks/hooks.json. A later part starts whenever the bound has not passed, and a part in progress at the bound runs to its end. A part is whole transcript records and has no upper size, so one long record is one part. The first part of an entry always runs, whatever its size and the time, so a transcript that cannot be split, or one under gitleaks, is redacted whole at the same prompt as before. A part, first or later, that runs past the host's limit ends the hook there without its output, as redacting the transcript whole did; nothing of that part is kept, and at the next pass it is the first part. So the hook's output is delivered when the part in progress at the bound ends inside the margin. The margin covers what follows the bound: the end of the part in progress, the records lock and the record write, the hook's output, the time the host's clock ran before the binary started, and at session start the notices after its drain. The bound counts from the binary's start and the host's limit from the wrapper's, so the margin assumes the wrapper started the binary well inside it; a first run that downloads the binary, or the dogfood route's build check, is outside that assumption, and a drain that uses its whole bound then can still lose the hook's output; no code holds the wrapper to it. When nothing the pass keeps could be continued by a later pass (a part could not be kept, the binary has a stamp unique to the process because it could not be read, or gitleaks reads the text whole), stopping saves nothing, so the entry runs to its record as before parts existed: the bound stops no later part, and the records lock is waited for as long as a capture waits. No shorter budget decides whether an entry is begun: the prompt drains one entry, and a session start that stopped beginning entries before its bound would leave staged a backlog it used to clear. The prompt step starts right after the root-commit lookup and runs beside the rules, notes, status and away block, and the hook waits for it on every return; a panic in it, or in the session start's drain, is recovered, named by its type, and the hook goes on. A kept part is used again only when the bytes it covers, the scanner it was redacted with (its pattern set, identity and augmenter), the home path, the running binary (the sha256 of its bytes, or a stamp unique to the process when those cannot be read) and, for a part that ended at the end of the text, the text's length are unchanged, so parts kept under another configuration are redone and the record is the one a single pass over the whole text writes. Two builds draining in turn therefore redo each other's parts, and a transcript neither can finish within one prompt waits for a session start that can, a drain run outside the hooks, or the builds to stop alternating. Why: the repository's security rule that a change must not leave anything unredacted on disk longer than before it. Under this rule a transcript is stored at the same prompt as before whenever its drain would have ended before the bound, and one that needs longer, which before ran into the host's limit at every prompt, lost the hook's output and was never finished, is finished over several prompts. The one case later than before is a transcript whose drain would have ended inside the margin, between the bound and the host's limit: it is finished at the next prompt, or, with two builds draining in turn, once a session start or a drain finishes it or the builds stop alternating. Keeping parts costs a little time of its own (a write per part, one digest of the binary) and the drain shares the processor with the rest of the hook, so that window opens slightly before the bound in the old drain's terms. No later part is held back on an estimate of its time, because an estimate cannot promise that a transcript is stored no later than before; a smaller margin would leave less room for the part in progress at the bound to end inside the host's limit. To reverse: a new decision naming what replaces the bound, with the security rule's comparison for every case.
