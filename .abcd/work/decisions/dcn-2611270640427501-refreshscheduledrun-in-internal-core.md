2026-11-27 — RefreshScheduledRun in internal/core/claim/scheduled.go keeps dropping the error from writing its failure record (_ = paths.keep(paths.failed, data)), and that leaves no wrong answer. The refresh runs detached with stderr sent to /dev/null, and the store is the only place the prompt reads, so a failure record that cannot be written has nowhere else to go. A probe on 2026-11-27 confirmed what the prompt does instead. Nothing false is kept: the dated green stays and the attempt stamp is newer than it. The prompt treats this as a refresh that kept nothing, the case scheduledPastDue already covers for a refresh that died before it could keep a failure. So once the answer is more than 7 hours old (the 1-hour refresh interval plus the 6-hour margin) it says the state is unknown and that a refresh started since kept nothing, and after 48 hours it calls the answer stale. Before 7 hours the prompt stays silent, as it does for a refresh lost to a closed laptop, where a written failure record would have said unknown from 1 hour. This rests on a stamp newer than the answer, and two cases keep none: a refresh run by abcd status, which stamps nothing, and a store unwritable from the start, where no stamp is ever kept. Both are covered by a line in the prompt's STATUS block, added with iss-2611051719396028: when a due prompt cannot keep its stamp, the block says the background refresh could not be started and its stamp could not be kept, with the home redacted, and a line goes to stderr for the operator. Since no stamp starts the cooldown, a store that stays unwritable shows that line on every due prompt, not once per 15 minutes. Where the store becomes writable again, the next due prompt stamps it, and the refresh that stamp starts falls under the rule above. abcd status also prints the not-read line to the person directly. To reverse this, have the read treat a due answer as unknown whenever a stamp more than scheduledRefreshDeath old is newer than both the answer and the failure record, dropping the 6-hour margin for that case, and pin it with a test that blocks the failure record's path.
